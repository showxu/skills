# Review Rubric

Use this rubric for design-side reviews of existing product prototypes,
wireframes, screenshots, or product surfaces.

## Task Fit

- The primary user task is visible within the first interaction.
- Required decisions appear in the order users naturally make them.
- The screen does not ask for information before explaining why it is needed.
- Frequent tasks are faster than rare or administrative tasks.

## Clarity

- The next action is visually and semantically clear.
- Labels describe outcomes, not internal system concepts.
- Controls with similar appearance have similar behavior.
- Destructive, public, expensive, or hard-to-reverse actions disclose
  consequence before commit.

## Efficiency

- Repeated workflows minimize re-entry, avoid unnecessary modals, and preserve
  context.
- Defaults are useful but reversible.
- Advanced options are discoverable without overwhelming the main path.
- Bulk actions include clear selection, preview, and recovery.

## Accessibility And Inclusion

- Interaction does not depend on color alone, hover alone, or precision input
  alone.
- Focus order follows task order.
- Error messages identify the field, cause, and next action.
- Touch targets and dense controls match the device and use context.
- Motion, timeout, and confirmation behavior do not block assistive use.

## Recovery

- The user can go back, cancel, retry, undo, or save progress when the task
  warrants it.
- Empty states provide a useful first action.
- Permission and offline states explain what is unavailable and what remains
  possible.
- Validation happens early enough to avoid avoidable rework.

## Handoff Quality

- Recommendations are tied to a specific screen, state, control, or step.
- Open questions are separated from decisions.
- Required state risks and product-decision handoffs are explicit.
- Design tradeoffs are stated when more than one reasonable path exists.
