# Voice And Terminology

## Voice

Voice is the product's stable way of speaking. Look for it in nearby product
copy, design-system docs, product docs, support material, and existing
terminology lists.

If no voice is defined, infer a working voice from the product context and mark
it as an assumption. Use concrete traits such as direct, calm, expert,
supportive, playful, restrained, precise, or warm.

## Tone

Tone changes by moment.

- Errors, privacy, billing, safety, and destructive actions: direct, specific,
  calm.
- Empty states and onboarding: warmer, but still purposeful.
- Success states: concise; personality is optional.
- Settings: utility over personality.

## Precedence

Apply this order:

1. Clarity: can the user understand the point?
2. Truth: does the copy preserve product facts and commitments?
3. Actionability: does the user know what to do next?
4. Voice: does it sound like this product?
5. Polish: can it be shorter or more consistent?

## Terminology

Use one term for one concept. Track repeated terms in a word list:

| Use | Avoid | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| workspace | project, space | Shared area where team content lives. |
| continue | next, proceed | Advance to the next step in a flow. |

Good word-list candidates:

- core product nouns
- repeated action labels
- plan, billing, permission, or account terms
- user-visible states such as synced, paused, archived, failed, or pending

## Localization-Friendly Writing

- Avoid idioms, jokes, and culturally specific phrases in core flows.
- Avoid fragile sentence fragments that depend on English word order.
- Leave room for text expansion.
- Keep placeholders meaningful and reorderable in later implementation.
